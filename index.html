Patterns worth tracking

Early hypotheses based on these four walks. They are not diagnoses, and the immediate cause of a behaviour is not always clear.

Wanting to leave the park

On the evening of 20 October and morning of 21 October, Nova wanted to head home after the poo bag was put in the bin. On the 21 October lunchtime walk, she also became reluctant to continue near the bench and headed for the exit. The repeated sequence is worth recording, but does not prove the bin or toileting caused it.

Children and changing distance

On the morning walk, Nova appeared comfortable with a child and mother at a distance, then growled and attempted to lunge when they were closer on the way home. Distance, approach direction and lead handling are useful details to log.

Tail wagging is not enough on its own

During the child encounter, Nova wagged her tail while growling and attempting to lunge. Read tail position alongside vocalisation, body movement, distance and recovery, rather than treating wagging alone as comfort.
